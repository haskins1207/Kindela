# Civora — Problem Aware Customer Avatar

**Product context:** Civora — the operating system for mission-driven nonprofits, combining donor and grant tracking (inbound and outbound), donated-goods inventory, fund accounting, volunteer management, and public transparency. *Every donation, grant, and good — connected and visible.*

**Framework:** Eugene Schwartz's Stages of Market Awareness (*Breakthrough Advertising*). This document covers the **Problem Aware** stage only.

---

## Stage of Awareness: Problem Aware

*She feels the friction every single day. The donor records live in one place, the grant pipeline in another, the resale store inventory in a third, and the volunteer schedule in a fourth. She knows children's goods are coming in faster than she can track them, that some get sold and some get routed to partner nonprofits, and that she can't cleanly show the public — or her funders — where the money and the goods actually went. She knows the problem is real and painful. She does not yet know that a single platform purpose-built for mission-driven resale nonprofits exists. She's hunting for relief, not yet for a named product.*

---

## A → Who Are They

**Name:** Karen "Kari" Whitfield

**Gender:** Female

**Job:** Executive Director / President of a children-focused nonprofit (mission: supporting children in crisis) that also runs a resale program — donated children's goods are either sold to generate unrestricted revenue or given to partner nonprofits serving kids on the ground. Annual operating budget ranges from $250K (small) up to several million (larger orgs). She owns development, operations, compliance, and board relations, and personally touches the books.

**Household Income:** $95,000 combined (she earns $62,000 personally — under-market for the responsibility she carries; spouse income fills the gap)

**Marital Status:** Married, with at least one child of her own — her personal "why" behind the mission.

**Education Level:** Bachelor's degree (Social Work, Nonprofit Management, or Business). Often working toward or holding a nonprofit leadership certificate. Self-taught on every piece of software she's forced to use.

**Top 3 Brands They Wear:** Talbots, LOFT, and items thrifted from her own resale store (which she wears with pride as a walking endorsement of the mission). Practical, professional, modest.

**1–2 Hobbies They Have:** Volunteering through her church or community group; gardening or long walks as her decompression ritual.

**Top 5 Favorite Movies:** *The Blind Side*, *Instant Family*, *Wonder*, *Hidden Figures*, *It's a Wonderful Life* — stories of vulnerable children and ordinary people who step up.

**Top 5 Favorite Books:** *Daring Greatly* (Brené Brown), *Toxic Charity* (Robert Lupton), *Good to Great and the Social Sectors* (Jim Collins), *The Purpose Driven Life* (Rick Warren), *Forces for Good* (Crutchfield & Grant).

**Top 5 Visited Websites:** Candid/GuideStar, Chronicle of Philanthropy, her bank's portal, Facebook (managing the org and resale store pages), and Google ("how to track in-kind donations and inventory for a nonprofit").

**Top 5 Relevant Social Media Influencers:** Vu Le (Nonprofit AF), Joan Garry (nonprofit leadership), Dana Snyder (nonprofit digital), Tim Sarrantonio (donor engagement), and regional community-foundation leaders she trusts.

## C → Why Are They

**Main Personality Traits:** Conscientious, fiercely mission-driven, deeply empathetic, chronically over-committed, allergic to waste, and quietly anxious about money and compliance. A doer who struggles to delegate because "it's faster if I just do it."

**5 Major Values They Hold:** Transparency/integrity, stewardship (every dollar and every donated item matters), protection of vulnerable children, accountability to donors and community, and faith-rooted compassion.

**2 Major Life Victories:**
1. Building or rescuing the organization and keeping kids served through a funding crisis.
2. Securing a transformational grant that funded a major on-the-ground program for children in crisis.

**2 Major Life Failures:**
1. Losing a grant renewal because she couldn't produce clean outcome and financial data fast enough.
2. An inventory or bookkeeping mess — donated goods unaccounted for, a reconciliation that wouldn't balance — that triggered a stressful audit and made her feel exposed in front of her board.

---

## D → Smart Market Questions

**What keeps them awake at night, eyes open, staring at the ceiling:** "Did I record that pallet of donated children's goods correctly — the part we sold versus the part we gave to our partner org? Is cash flow going to cover payroll? If a donor, a journalist, or the IRS asked me to prove exactly where every dollar and every donated item went, could I actually show them cleanly?"

**What are they secretly afraid of in life:** Being exposed as disorganized or unprofessional and having that destroy the public trust she's spent years building — trust that directly translates into kids getting served. That she's holding the whole operation together with spreadsheets, sticky notes, and luck.

**What are they angry about, and who are they angry at:** Angry that software vendors offer only two options — bloated enterprise platforms priced for huge foundations, or generic retail POS and accounting tools that have no concept of in-kind donations, fund accounting, or routing goods to other nonprofits. Angry at the false choice between affordable-but-useless and capable-but-unaffordable. Frustrated with funders who demand polished impact reporting but fund zero overhead to produce it.

**Top 3 frustrations they feel every day:**
1. Stitching together five disconnected systems — POS, QuickBooks, a donor database, a grants calendar, and a volunteer sign-up sheet — none of which talk to each other.
2. Re-keying the same data over and over.
3. Being unable to instantly answer "how are we doing, and can we prove it?" for the board or the public.

**Biggest secret desire in life:** To run a children's nonprofit so transparent and well-run that donors trust it on sight, the team isn't burned out, and she can finally pour her energy into the mission instead of the machinery.

**Built-in bias to how they make decisions:** Risk-averse and budget-first. She trusts other EDs far more than sales reps. She needs to *see* it work and *believe* it was built for someone exactly like her, not for a corporation.

**Common words or language unique to them:** "In-kind donations," "restricted vs. unrestricted funds," "fund accounting," "donor acknowledgment letters," "Form 990," "overhead ratio," "impact reporting," "outbound granting," "inventory shrinkage," "board deck," "shoestring," "wearing all the hats."

**Top 3 complaints about existing solutions:**
1. "It's either way too expensive or way too basic."
2. "Nothing handles a resale store AND donor tracking AND grants AND giving goods to other nonprofits — I have to duct-tape it together myself."
3. "Building a credible, public-facing report takes hours and still doesn't look trustworthy enough for funders."

---

## E → Going Deep

**Top 3 Dominant Negative Emotions:** Overwhelm, anxiety (financial and compliance), and a gnawing sense of inadequacy ("everyone else seems to have this figured out").

**Top 3 Dominant Positive Emotions From Solving This Problem:** Relief (the weight lifts off her shoulders), pride (she can finally showcase clean, transparent numbers and impact), and confidence (she walks into the board meeting fully prepared).

**Top 3 Beliefs They Hold About The World:** Good organizations deserve to thrive, not just survive. Transparency builds trust, and trust builds funding. The frontline nonprofits doing the real work for children are chronically under-resourced and overlooked.

**Biggest Lifestyle Desire:** To stop carrying the stress home — to have evenings and weekends back, knowing the books are clean, the inventory is accounted for, the grants are tracked, and the public can see exactly how their generosity reaches kids.

---

## F → Purchasing Habits

**Top 3 Decision Triggers:**
1. A painful event (failed audit, lost grant, board pressure for better reporting).
2. A trusted peer ED saying "this changed everything for us."
3. A clear demonstration that one platform replaces the five tools she's currently stitching together.

**Prior Purchases For This Pain:** QuickBooks / QuickBooks for Nonprofits, a generic retail POS (Square or Lightspeed), a low-cost donor CRM (Bloomerang, Little Green Light, or DonorPerfect), free spreadsheets, and SignUpGenius or similar for volunteers.

**Price Tolerance For Offer:** Sensitive but realistic. Will balk at $500+/month enterprise pricing, but can justify roughly $50–$250/month *if* it provably consolidates multiple subscriptions and saves staff hours. Strongly values tiered nonprofit pricing and a genuine free trial.

**Time Horizon Of Solution:** Wants relief now but is realistic about implementation — expects to be fully running within 30–90 days, ideally with migration help so she isn't re-entering years of donor and inventory data alone.

---

## G → Primary Wants

**Wants to gain:** A single source of truth — sales, donated-goods inventory, donors, grants (inbound and outbound), and volunteers in one place — plus credible, public-facing transparency.

**Wants to be:** Seen as the leader of a trustworthy, professionally run, transparent organization that protects children.

**Wants to do:** Spend her time on mission and people, not data entry and reconciliation.

**Wants to save:** Money (consolidated subscriptions), time (no more double entry), and her sanity.

**Wants to avoid:** Audits, compliance errors, lost grants, untracked inventory, donor distrust, board embarrassment, and burnout.

---

## H → Empathy Map

**Seeing:** A cluttered desk, a dozen browser tabs, a QuickBooks screen that won't match the POS report, bins of donated children's goods waiting to be logged, and an email from a funder requesting an impact report by Friday.

**Thinking:** "There has to be a better way. Why is this so hard? How does everyone else manage all of this without losing their minds?"

**Hearing:** Board members asking "what's our actual financial position?", funders asking for outcomes data, partner nonprofits asking what goods are coming their way, and staff asking questions she's too slammed to answer.

**Feeling:** Stretched thin, anxious, fiercely dedicated, and quietly afraid she's one mistake away from a crisis that hurts the kids she serves.

**Saying:** "I just need everything in one place." "I wish I could show the community exactly where their donations go." "We can't afford the fancy systems the big nonprofits use."

**Doing:** Manually exporting and re-keying data between systems, building reports the night before board meetings, chasing receipts and inventory counts, and Googling affordable workarounds at 10 p.m.
