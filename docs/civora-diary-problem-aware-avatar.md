# The Diary of Kari Whitfield

*Journal entries from the perspective of Civora's Problem Aware avatar, across three moments: before using the product, during first use, and after.*

---

## Entry One — Before

*11:47 p.m. Tuesday*

The house finally went quiet an hour ago, and here I am again at the kitchen table, the laptop fan whirring like it's as tired as I am. There's a cold mug of tea by my elbow with a film on top. I keep meaning to drink it. I keep meaning to do a lot of things.

I had a dream last night — or maybe it was just the thought that wouldn't leave — that someone stood up at the gala, a donor in a nice blazer, and asked me, *"Where exactly did my money go?"* And I opened my mouth and nothing came out. Just static. Because the honest answer is that the money is scattered across six places that don't speak to each other, like children who've stopped talking after a fight. QuickBooks says one thing. The POS says another. The donor spreadsheet — God, the donor spreadsheet — has a row I'm pretty sure I duplicated in March and never fixed.

There are bins in the back room right now. Three of them. Donated children's coats, little sneakers, a stuffed rabbit with one ear. Beautiful things. Things that could keep a kid warm or hand a grieving family one ordinary good day. And I don't know — I genuinely *do not know* — which of those went to the resale floor and which got routed to the partner org last week. The knowing slipped through my fingers somewhere between the loading dock and 1 a.m.

My chest does this thing now. A tightening, right behind the sternum, like a fist closing slow. It shows up around grant-deadline weeks and it doesn't leave until I've cried in the car at least once. I'm so tired of the car cries.

And underneath the tired is the fear, which is uglier. I'm afraid someone will look closely and see that the whole thing — this organization I bled for — is held up with spreadsheets and sticky notes and prayer. That I'm not a *real* executive director. That I'm a woman with a heart too big for her competence, faking it until the audit catches me.

The board meets Thursday. They'll ask for the numbers. I'll stay up tomorrow night building a report that looks confident and isn't, and I'll smile through it, and no one will know my hands were shaking under the table.

There has to be a better way. There *has* to be. I just can't find it at midnight with a cold mug of tea.

---

## Entry Two — During First Use

*9:23 p.m. Thursday (a different Thursday — two weeks later)*

I'm almost afraid to write this down in case it isn't real.

I sat down tonight expecting the usual war. I had my coffee, my dread, my mental list of which systems would betray me first. I logged in to the new thing — the one Diane swore changed her life, and I rolled my eyes when she said it, because everyone says that.

But.

I entered the bins. The actual bins. The coats and the little sneakers and the one-eared rabbit. And it asked me — *it asked me* — how much went to the resale floor and how much went to the partner org. Like it already understood that those are two different rivers running out of the same donation. No one has ever built a thing that understood that before. I sat there for a second just staring at the screen, this stupid lump rising in my throat.

The donor record was already linked to the acknowledgment letter. The grant pipeline had a little timeline, the deadlines just *sitting* there, calm, not lunging at me from a calendar I forgot to check.

My hands weren't shaking. I noticed that around 9 p.m. — my hands were just... still. Resting on the keys. I almost didn't recognize them.

It's not perfect yet. I have years of old data to bring over and the thought of that migration makes the old fist start to close again. There's a learning curve and I clicked the wrong thing twice and muttered at it. I'm not naïve. I've been burned by shiny tools before that promised the moon and handed me a heavier spreadsheet.

But for the first time in longer than I can remember, I felt something flicker that wasn't dread. Something small and warm and almost frightening in its unfamiliarity. Like the first deep breath after coming up from underwater.

Hope, maybe. I'm scared to name it. I'm naming it anyway.

---

## Entry Three — After

*8:05 p.m. Wednesday*

I closed the laptop tonight before nine. Before *nine.* And I went and sat on the porch with Maddie and we counted three fireflies and she fell asleep on my arm and I just... stayed there. Whole. Present. Not half of me chewing on a reconciliation in the background.

The board meeting last week — I want to remember exactly how it felt, so future-Kari can read this on a hard night and believe her.

A board member asked the question. *The* question. "What's our actual financial position, and can we show the public where the donations went?" And instead of the static, instead of the closing fist, I turned the screen around. I showed them the dashboard. Revenue from the store, real-time. The grants we've written, inbound and outbound, laid out clean as a made bed. The donated goods, traced from the loading dock to the resale floor or to the partner org serving kids across town. Every river accounted for.

There was a silence. Then the chair said, quietly, "Kari, this is extraordinary." And I didn't deflect it. I didn't say *oh it's nothing.* I just let it land, because for once it was true and I had earned it.

The fear that lived behind my sternum for years — that someone would look closely and find a fraud — it's quiet now. Not because I got perfect, but because there's nothing hiding in the dark anymore. The light's on. Anyone can look. *Please,* look. That's the whole point.

I think about the woman at that kitchen table with her cold tea and her car cries and I want to reach back through time and put my hand on her shoulder and tell her: it's coming. The breath you can't take yet — it's coming. You're not faking it. You're just doing it with the wrong tools, in the dark, alone, and none of that is the same as not being enough.

The rabbit with one ear went to a little girl last month. I know that now. I can *prove* it. And somehow that one small certainty has made everything feel possible again.

Lights off. Bed before ten. The books are clean and so, finally, is my mind.
